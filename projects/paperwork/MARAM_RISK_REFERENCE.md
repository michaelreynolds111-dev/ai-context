# MARAM Risk Assessment Reference

**Purpose:** Reference card for the Paperwork agent. Defines the risk domains
used in the Risk Assessment table (CASP5) and referenced in CASP4 formulations.
Extracted from the service's risk assessment framework.

**Classification:** [SENSITIVE] — references youth mental health risk
assessment structure. Sanitized — no client data.

---

## Risk Assessment Table Structure

The Risk Assessment table appears in the CASP5 Psychiatric Progress Note. It
has 17 rows (risk domains) and 7 columns (risk ratings + remarks).

### Columns

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Past | The risk has occurred in the past but is not currently active |
| Unknown or Unable to be assessed | The clinician does not have enough information to rate this risk |
| No Clinical Risk | The risk domain is not relevant to this client |
| Low | The risk is present but well-managed or infrequent |
| Mod | The risk is present and requires active monitoring or intervention |
| High | The risk is active, severe, or imminent — requires urgent action |
| Remarks | Free text — is the risk chronic, acute, or acute on chronic? Any context needed to understand the rating |

### Rating Rules

- Any **Mod** or **High** rating **requires a remark** in the Remarks column
- Remarks should specify whether the risk is **chronic** (long-standing
  background risk), **acute** (new or escalated), or **acute on chronic** (a
  new escalation of a long-standing risk)

### Risk Domains (Rows)

| # | Risk Domain | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Suicidality | Suicidal ideation, plans, intent, attempts. Current and historical. |
| 2 | Self-harm | Non-suicidal self-injury, cutting, burning, other self-harm behaviours. |
| 3 | Aggression | Violence or aggression toward others. Physical and verbal. |
| 4 | Social Isolation | Withdrawal from social contact, lack of supports, loneliness. |
| 5 | Alcohol and other Drug Issues | Substance use — frequency, quantity, impact on functioning, polysubstance use. |
| 6 | Inappropriate Sexual Behaviour | Sexual behaviour that is harmful to self or others, or contextually inappropriate. |
| 7 | Cognitive Impairment | Intellectual disability, acquired brain injury, cognitive decline affecting decision-making. |
| 8 | Serious Medical Condition | Physical health conditions requiring management — epilepsy, diabetes, cardiac conditions. |
| 9 | Non-compliance | Not engaging with treatment, not taking medication, not attending appointments. |
| 10 | Cultural Risk | Risks related to cultural identity, discrimination, disconnection from community. |
| 11 | Vulnerability to Exploitation from Others | Being taken advantage of financially, sexually, or otherwise by others. |
| 12 | Abuse/Neglect or Exploitation of Others | The client as perpetrator of abuse, neglect, or exploitation toward others. |
| 13 | Nutrition/Dietary Intake | Disordered eating, food insecurity, significant weight changes. |
| 14 | Sleep Hygiene | Reversed sleep cycle, insomnia, hypersomnia, sleep affected by substance use. |
| 15 | ADL capacity/Self Neglect | Activities of daily living — hygiene, self-care, maintaining living environment. |
| 16 | Homelessness | Housing instability, risk of eviction, couch surfing, rough sleeping. |
| 17 | Falls | Risk of falls — relevant for clients with mobility issues, medication side effects, or seizure disorders. |

### Below the Table

After the risk assessment table, the following must be completed:

| Field | Options | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Early episode psychosis | Yes / No / Possible-Prodromal | Indicates whether the client is in an early phase of psychotic illness |
| Forensic History | Yes / No / Unknown + details | Any involvement with the justice system |
| Family Violence History | Yes / No + details | Any history of family violence — as victim, perpetrator, or witness |
| Factors indicating uncertainty | Free text | Anything that makes the risk assessment less reliable |
| Level of risk highly changeable | Free text | If the risk picture can shift rapidly |
| Significant protective/resilience factors | Free text | What's keeping the client safe or well |

## Risk Concepts Used in CASP4 Formulations

The CASP4 formulation references risk in narrative form, not in a table. Key
concepts:

### Chronic vs Acute Risk

- **Chronic risk:** A long-standing background risk that is part of the client's
  presentation over time. Stated in the static summary paragraph. Example:
  "Chronic risks include vulnerability to exploitation, psychosis relapse, and
  housing instability."
- **Acute risk:** A new or escalated risk that has emerged in the reporting
  period. Stated in the update. Example: "A FVISS request returned a concerning
  picture of [PARTNER]'s history: multiple L17s, strangulation of a sibling,
  threats to kill."
- **Acute on chronic:** A new escalation of a long-standing risk. Example: A
  client with chronic housing instability who has now received an eviction
  notice.

### Risk Corroboration

- Risk information from the client: "[CLIENT] denied substance use"
- Risk information corroborated: "corroborated by DS Nursing"
- Risk information from external sources: "A FVISS request returned..."
- Risk ruled out: "showed no signs of psychotic symptoms or suicidal ideation"

### Risk and Ambivalence

- The client's mixed feelings about risk are documented without resolution:
  "[CLIENT] not intending to end relationship at this point, confused and
  ambivalent"
- The clinician's concern is stated separately from the client's position
- The formulation does not force a conclusion — it presents the picture as it
  is

### Protective Factors

- Family relationships improving and increasingly supportive
- Engagement with services (NDIS, YPARC, CNV)
- Good self-insight ("she noted she finds interactions over a table hard and
  prefers side by side")
- Working toward goals (P plates, TAFE, employment)
- Medication stabilisation
- A plan in place for ongoing contact

## Risk Documentation in Different Document Types

| Document | How risk is documented |
|---|---|
| CASP5 Progress Note | Full Risk Assessment table with all 17 rows rated + below-table fields completed |
| CASP4 Formulation | Narrative — chronic risks in static summary, acute risks in update, protective factors woven throughout |
| Handover | Key risks only — what the receiving clinician needs to know immediately |
| Referral letter | Relevant risks for the receiving service — not the full table |
| NDIS report | Functional impact of risks — how they affect daily living and support needs |
| Professional correspondence | Risk relevant to the recipient's role — selective, not comprehensive |
