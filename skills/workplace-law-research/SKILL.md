# Workplace Law Research

## When to use
- Researching employee rights and employer obligations under Australian law
- Fair Work Act 2009 questions (unfair dismissal, general protections, adverse action)
- Modern award interpretation and coverage
- Enterprise agreement compliance
- Redundancy (genuine vs sham)
- Underpayment and wage theft analysis
- Discrimination (FWA, state-based, Australian Human Rights Commission)
- WHS/OHS obligations and incident response
- Fair Work Commission proceedings

## Hard rules
- **Always cite the legislative provision, award clause, or case name** for every legal proposition.
- **Distinguish between federal and state jurisdictions** — Fair Work Act covers national system employers; some states retain separate systems for state public servants.
- **Note currency.** Awards and legislation change. Flag the date of any source used and note if it may have been updated.
- **Distinguish legal advice from legal information.** This skill provides research and analysis, not legal advice for a specific matter. For a specific matter, recommend seeking legal advice.
- **Don't overstate certainty.** Employment law outcomes are highly fact-dependent.

## Key sources to prioritise
- Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth)
- National Employment Standards (NES) — FWA Pt 2-2
- Fair Work Commission decisions (fwc.gov.au)
- Fair Work Ombudsman guidance (fairwork.gov.au)
- Australian Human Rights Commission (humanrights.gov.au)
- Relevant modern awards (check coverage before applying)
- State WHS legislation where applicable

## Process
1. Identify the jurisdiction (national system vs state).
2. Identify the applicable legislative framework and relevant award/agreement.
3. Research the relevant provisions with citations.
4. Apply to the stated facts, noting where facts are assumed or uncertain.
5. Note any recent case law that is directly on point.

## Output format
- Summary finding with legislative citation
- Key provisions quoted or paraphrased with section references
- Relevant cases: name, [year] court abbreviation, brief holding
- Any open questions or jurisdiction risks flagged clearly
- If time-sensitive (e.g. unfair dismissal 21-day limit), flag the deadline prominently
